## Design Context

### Users

Commercial-diving training planners and supervisors use this offline workstation before training exercises to assemble exposure assumptions, inspect deterministic model output, compare alternatives, and record human review. It is not used underwater or as a live life-support control.

### Brand Personality

Disciplined, legible, cautious. The interface should help reviewers feel oriented and in control without implying clinical or operational authority.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light industrial operations console inspired by printed dive logs and instrument panels: dense but calm layouts, cool neutral surfaces, sea-green status cues, amber warnings, strong tabular alignment, and restrained motion. Avoid cinematic ocean imagery, medical styling, neon dashboards, gradients, oversized marketing typography, and decorative cards.

### Design Principles

1. Keep the training-only safety boundary visible on every business page.
2. Put input evidence, assumptions, versions, and review state ahead of decorative metrics.
3. Never encode risk by color alone; pair every cue with explicit text and iconography.
4. Make sequencing and model provenance easy to scan, compare, and audit.
5. Adapt dense tables into readable stacked records on narrow screens without hiding critical actions.
